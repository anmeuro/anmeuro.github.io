Acts 2 — Pentecost and the Birth of the Church

When the day of Pentecost came, they were all together in one place. Acts 2:1

Acts 2 opens with the promised Spirit arriving in power. Tongues of fire, a sound like a rushing wind, and disciples speaking in the languages of every nation gathered in Jerusalem — the scattering at Babel is answered by a gathering at Pentecost.

Peter's Sermon

Peter stands and preaches the first Christian sermon, tracing the events back to the prophet Joel and forward to the resurrection of Jesus. His argument is simple: the Jesus they crucified is both Lord and Christ, and this outpouring of the Spirit is proof.

  • The Spirit's coming fulfills Joel's prophecy — sons and daughters, young and old, all receive the Spirit.
  • Peter's sermon centers on the resurrection as God's vindication of Jesus.
  • Three thousand are added to the church in a single day.

The Shape of the Early Church

The chapter closes with a picture of what this new community looked like: devoted to the apostles' teaching, to fellowship, to breaking bread, and to prayer — with everything held in common.

  • Reflect: What would it look like for our own community to be devoted to these same four things?
  • Pray: Ask God for a fresh outpouring of the Spirit in our own church and campus ministry.
