Acts 10:39–48 — Peter's Sermon to Cornelius

While Peter was still speaking, the Holy Spirit fell on everyone who heard the message. Acts 10:44

The group completed Acts 10:39–48, working through a detailed bilingual exposition of Peter's sermon to Cornelius. Peter had entered a Gentile home — a boundary neither Jew nor Gentile normally crossed — and both sides needed to be transformed before the gospel could cross that line. The study identified five core elements of the gospel as Peter preached it, then traced what happened next: the Spirit fell on the Gentiles before baptism, and a genuinely mixed fellowship began.

Five Core Elements of Peter's Gospel

  • Eyewitness testimony. Peter and the disciples personally saw Jesus' teaching, miracles, death, and resurrection — not secondhand reports. Over 500 witnessed the risen Christ, anchoring the resurrection as a verifiable historical event.
  • The death of Jesus — "hung on a tree." The group noted a translation detail: the Greek word is "tree/wood," not "cross," and the Chinese rendering (挂在木头上) captures this. Under Mosaic law, being hung on a tree signified a curse — language that predates Roman crucifixion and would have struck first-century hearers as an image of condemnation, not just execution. Jesus bore that curse in place of others.
  • The resurrection — vindication and victory. God raising Jesus declares him innocent and righteous, defeats death itself, and opens resurrection to all who believe. Peter and the disciples ate with the risen Jesus, underscoring a bodily, historical event — not a vision or metaphor.
  • Jesus as Lord of all — a challenge to Caesar. In Rome, "Lord" (Kyrios) was Caesar's title. Peter's claim that Jesus is Lord of all nations was a direct challenge to imperial authority — pointed for Cornelius, a Roman officer, and still relevant for believers navigating civic loyalty and spiritual allegiance today.
  • Jesus as judge of the living and the dead. This isn't salvation by works — every life is recorded and will be accounted for. For believers this means reward, not condemnation; the group referenced the wedding guest without proper garments (Matthew) as a warning against passive, fruitless faith.

The Command to Preach

Preaching the gospel is not optional or a matter of spiritual gifting — it is a command, framed by the Great Commission as the primary mission of every believer. The group corrected a common misconception: a Christian's role is to faithfully proclaim; whether someone believes is God's work, not the evangelist's responsibility.

The Spirit Poured Out on the Gentiles

While Peter was still speaking, the Spirit fell on everyone listening — the first clear, large-scale outpouring on uncircumcised Gentiles in Acts, witnessed by multiple people at once and therefore undeniable. This capped Peter's own gradual transformation: from the vision of unclean food, to entering Cornelius' home, to now witnessing the Spirit fall on Gentiles. Six Jewish believers accompanied him — providentially, since a single witness would not have been credible when Peter later reported this to the Jerusalem elders.

The Order of Salvation — and Fellowship Across the Barrier

The sequence in this passage is instructive: belief, then the Spirit received, then baptism. The group firmly rejected the view that baptism itself saves — these Gentiles were already saved and Spirit-filled before the water. (Discussed edge case: a dying person who believes and confesses but cannot be baptized in time is still saved; faith alone is the criterion.) Afterward, the Gentiles invited Peter to stay with them for several days — itself revolutionary, since Jews and Gentiles had never shared meals and living space this way. His acceptance was full recognition of these Gentile believers as brothers and sisters in Christ.
