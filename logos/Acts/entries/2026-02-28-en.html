Acts 1 — The Ascension, the Promise of the Spirit & the New Apostle

But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth. Acts 1:8

The group's first full study of Acts 1 covered the ascension and the promise of the Spirit, the choosing of a replacement apostle, and questions about God's sovereignty and human free will.

The Mount of Olives

Jesus chose the Mount of Olives for the ascension for several reasons: it sits about 2 km from Jerusalem at higher elevation, conveniently placing the Spirit's coming descent in Jerusalem itself; Jerusalem carries deep symbolism as the Holy City and City of David; the Mount was Jesus' frequent place of prayer with the Father; and it was also where he was betrayed and suffered — Gethsemane ("olive press") symbolizing the pressure he endured for our redemption.

The Prayer Gathering

About 120 people gathered after the ascension, including the 11 apostles, Jesus' mother, and his brothers — notably, brothers who had not believed in him before, but were transformed in faith after the resurrection and ascension.

The Spirit's Work in Peter, Before Pentecost

The Spirit was already at work in the disciples — especially Peter — before Pentecost, in two ways: bringing the indwelling of salvation, and empowering for gospel work. Peter's wise use of Old Testament scripture and his grasp of Judas' betrayal and Jesus' prophecies came from the Spirit's revelation, showing God's plan at work.

Luke the Historian

The parenthetical explanations scattered through Acts 1 are Luke's own annotations for his Gentile reader, Theophilus — Jerusalem believers already knew these details, but Gentile readers needed them spelled out. Luke's physician's background shows in his detailed description of Judas' death.

God's Sovereignty and the Fulfillment of Prophecy

Peter's quotation of the Psalms in verse 20 illustrates God's plan over future events — likened to a director arranging a script, pre-arranging the course of history.

Choosing a New Apostle

Two conditions governed the choice: the candidate had to have been with Jesus from John's baptism through the ascension, and to have personally witnessed the resurrection. Leadership ability comes not from education or personal experience but from the Spirit's revelation — God uses people based on willingness to follow, not credentials. Two candidates, Barsabbas and Matthias, were put forward; the choice was settled through prayer and the casting of lots.

Who Were the Apostles?

"Apostle" wasn't limited to the Twelve — it included Timothy, Paul, and others, roughly 50 in total. Their core role in the early church was to lay its foundation, at a time when there was no New Testament yet and much confusion over doctrine and structure — making their role especially crucial. This also marks a shift in how God reveals himself: in the Old Testament, revelation came largely through external means like prayer and casting lots; in the New Testament, the Spirit's coming lets God dwell directly with people, making his will known through inner revelation.

Sovereignty and Free Will

God knows everything and holds infinite wisdom and power, but he does not control every decision — people are not tools operated like machines, but co-workers with God, not robots. We participate through prayer and obedience, and God reshapes us through sanctification, not by making us automatic. Redemption isn't merely educational; it requires sanctification and action — God forges life through difficulty and valleys, the way gold is refined, and calls Christians to be his friends and co-workers, understanding his heart through Scripture and prayer.

Time and Eternity

Using the analogy of a football match: people are bound by time and must strive within the game, while God transcends time and already knows its outcome — but that doesn't remove the need for human effort along the way. Life moves in an upward spiral, with peaks and valleys, but an overall direction that draws closer to God.
