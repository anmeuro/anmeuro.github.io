Acts 7 — Stephen's Speech & Martyrdom

Look, I see heaven open and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God. Acts 7:56

This study covered the whole of Acts 7 — Stephen's historic speech before the Sanhedrin and his martyrdom. Using Abraham, Joseph, and Moses as a framework, Stephen systematically shows that God is not confined to the temple, and traces Israel's long history of rebelling against God and rejecting the very people he sent — connecting that pattern directly to the rejection of Jesus. The speech ends with Stephen stoned to death, a death that became a historical turning point for the gospel's spread across the whole world.

Stephen's Arrest

Stephen was one of the seven deacons, a lay leader rather than an apostle, full of the Spirit and wisdom. He proclaimed Christ boldly in the synagogues and repeatedly won debates with the religious leaders — who, filled with jealousy, fabricated charges that he opposed Moses, the Law, and God itself. These were the identical charges once used against Jesus, a familiar tactic for suppressing anyone who threatened established power. And as Jesus chose silence rather than self-defense, Stephen used the moment instead to preach — delivering the longest speech recorded in Acts.

Abraham: God Beyond the Temple

God called Abraham while he was still in Mesopotamia — before Canaan, long before any temple existed. The Israelites had come to believe God dwelt only in the temple, effectively making the temple itself an idol; yet God revealed himself to Abraham, a man of Gentile background, outside the promised land and before any temple was built — showing God as God of the whole world, not merely of Israel.

Joseph: Rejected, Then a Savior

Sold into Egypt by his own jealous brothers — the ancestors of Israel's twelve tribes — Joseph was nonetheless kept by God, given favor and wisdom, and raised to become second only to Pharaoh, saving Israel and its neighbors from famine. This marks the start of Israel's pattern of rejecting those God sends, and Joseph prefigures Jesus directly: rejected by his own people, made a savior, exalted by God after rejection — Joseph saving his family and nation, Jesus saving all humanity.

Moses: Rejected, Then Sent

Born during Israel's oppression and raised by Pharaoh's daughter, Moses at forty tried to defend his own people, only to be rejected with the words, "Who made you ruler and judge over us?" He fled to Midian — Gentile land — where God appeared to him in the burning bush, demonstrating again that God is present everywhere. Moses himself foreshadows Christ: God's promise that "God will raise up for you a prophet like me" points forward to the Messiah. Yet while Moses received the Ten Commandments on Sinai, the people were already making and worshiping a golden calf — a complete rejection of God. Israel's history is full of idolatry, and even the temple, God's own gift, eventually became an idol in their hearts.

The Tabernacle and Temple

The tabernacle let Israel experience God's presence — a forerunner of the temple, symbolizing God dwelling among his people, not confined to a building. "The Most High does not dwell in houses made by human hands" (v. 48); heaven is his throne and earth his footstool, the Creator and Sustainer of all things. Israel turned the temple into an idol and reduced faith to empty ritual — not unlike what we might call "Sunday Christians" today, people with the outward form of religion but no real relationship with God.

The Climax: A Direct Accusation

Stephen delivers one of Scripture's strongest rebukes: "You stiff-necked people, uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit." Their ancestors persecuted and killed the prophets who foretold the Messiah; they themselves betrayed and murdered the Righteous One; they received the Law through angels and still failed to keep it. Israel's whole history is a history of rejecting the ones God sent — Joseph, Moses, the prophets, and finally the Messiah himself. Stephen's arrest is simply the same tragic pattern continuing.

Theme: God Is Everywhere

The recurring truth through the whole speech: God is not confined to any place or building — he can be with us at home, on the road, in China, in Africa, anywhere. Human beings naturally want to make God tangible and controllable through idols or religious systems, but that reduces faith to empty religion, absent real relationship. God desires genuine relationship — Father, Friend, Redeemer — and, as Romans teaches, creation's own beauty reveals his glory and sustaining power.

The Martyrdom of Stephen

Hearing this, the people were furious and gnashed their teeth — the truth exposed their disobedience, and rather than repent, they raged. They dragged Stephen outside the city and stoned him, laying their garments at the feet of a young man named Saul. Filled with the Spirit as he was stoned, Stephen saw heaven opened and Jesus standing at God's right hand — notably standing, where elsewhere Scripture describes him seated, symbolizing Christ rising to welcome and honor his first martyr. Stephen prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit," then knelt and cried out, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them" — closely echoing Jesus' own prayer from the cross, "Father, forgive them." Stephen lived out a truly Christlike death.

A Turning Point

Saul was present, apparently with some authority over the persecution, and witnessed the execution — the exact reason the witnesses laid garments at his feet isn't certain, whether custom or simply so they could move freely to stone Stephen, though he did not personally throw stones. This same Saul would later become Paul, chosen by God to carry the gospel to the Gentile world. Stephen's death triggered a great persecution, scattering believers from Jerusalem and beginning the spread of the gospel into Samaria and to the ends of the earth — fulfilling Acts 1:8. Though only a deacon and lay leader, Stephen's martyrdom was the first in church history, opening a new chapter in Acts.
