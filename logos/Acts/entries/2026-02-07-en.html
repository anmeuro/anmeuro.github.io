Introduction to Acts — Structure, Authorship & the Kingdom of God

Acts is, in a sense, still being written today — the same Spirit who worked through the early church still empowers believers now. Overview session

This opening session laid the foundation for the whole book of Acts before starting on chapter 1. Acts sits as the bridge between the four Gospels and the Epistles, giving the historical context for how the early church began in Jerusalem and spread through the Greek world into Europe.

Structure of Acts

The book divides into two major sections: chapters 1–12 focus on Israel and the Jewish context, while chapters 13–28 cover the expansion into the Gentile world. The middle section bridges both Jewish and Samaritan contexts, tracing the gospel's progressive spread outward from Jerusalem.

Luke's Authorship

  • Luke, a Gentile physician, wrote both the Gospel of Luke and Acts as a two-volume work — he was the only non-Jewish Gospel writer.
  • He served as a careful historian and researcher; in chapter 16 he gives a firsthand account of traveling with Paul.
  • Acts may carry an apologetic purpose — possibly written to defend Paul during his imprisonment in Rome while awaiting trial.
  • Luke and Acts must be read together as one continuous narrative, both addressed to Theophilus: the work of Jesus on earth, followed by the work of the Spirit through the early church.

The Kingdom of God

The disciples initially misunderstood the Kingdom, expecting a political restoration of Israel from Roman rule. Three key truths correct this: the Kingdom is not political in nature; it is not merely a post-death concept; and it refers to God's reign, entered upon accepting Christ. This Kingdom is "already but not yet" — established now through the Spirit's work, but awaiting full manifestation at Christ's return. Living in it requires accepting Jesus as Lord and King, and pursuing ongoing sanctification.

Acts as "The Acts of the Holy Spirit"

Acts could rightly be called the Acts of the Holy Spirit, since it shows how the Spirit works in and through believers — the Spirit of Truth, who lets believers experience the Kingdom now even though it remains invisible, until Christ returns to bring the new heaven and new earth. Though Acts has only 28 chapters, the Spirit's work continues: modern believers keep building God's Kingdom by spreading the gospel, empowered by the same Spirit who worked through the early church.

Where Is the Kingdom?

In response to a question about the Kingdom's location in Revelation: the Kingdom is a spiritual reality, not a physical place. It exists in China, in America, in every believer's heart — Christians already live in it while existing between two worlds. The New Testament emphasizes present reality over future eschatology: believers are called to live as citizens of the Kingdom now, focused on the journey of following Christ today rather than only the final destination.
