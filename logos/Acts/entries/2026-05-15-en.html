Acts 4:27-35 — Shaken by Prayer & a Shared Life

After they prayed, the place where they were meeting was shaken. And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke the word of God boldly. Acts 4:31

This study followed how the early church responded to persecution: after praying, God answered immediately — the meeting place was shaken, the believers were filled with the Spirit, and they proclaimed the resurrection with new boldness. It also revealed their unique shared life: like a family, holding possessions in common and distributing according to need — a model belonging to a specific historical moment, but whose core spirit of unity, love, and voluntary giving still stands as an example.

God's Sovereignty and Human Responsibility

Jesus' crucifixion was part of God's predetermined plan, foretold in the Old Testament — yet Judas remained personally responsible for his betrayal. This is one of Scripture's paradoxes that transcends full human understanding: God's sovereignty and human free will coexist, as with Pharaoh, whose heart Scripture says was both hardened by God and hardened by himself. Judas already struggled with greed, often stealing from the money bag; choosing to betray Jesus was his own choice. Likewise, Romans 1 teaches that God's power and nature are revealed through creation, giving everyone a fair opportunity to know him — even without hearing the gospel, conscience and general revelation point to God's existence. Believers must not use predestination as an excuse to avoid evangelism, or as an excuse for others' unbelief.

How the Early Church Prayed

Gathering to pray, the disciples first exalted God's sovereignty as Creator and Lord over all things, quoting the Psalms: even as nations rage and kings unite against him, God remains victorious. They did not ask for persecution to be removed or enemies destroyed — only for greater boldness to keep preaching. Their prayer did not ask for changed circumstances but for strength to stand firm within them; not for personal safety but for God's name to be glorified; any healing or deliverance was asked for the sake of exalting Jesus' name, not their own. The answer came immediately: the place was shaken, a visible sign of God's presence echoing Old Testament theophanies of earthquake, light, and fire, and they were filled with the Spirit in a corporate outpouring resembling Pentecost — speaking God's word boldly as a result.

A Repeating Pattern

A pattern recurs through Acts: a small group begins preaching; persecution quickly follows; believers gather to pray, not for persecution to stop but for God to display his power; God answers through the Spirit's filling, miracles, and healing; the disciples grow bolder and the gospel spreads further. This same pattern shows up throughout church history — in the Reformation under Luther and Calvin, and in many countries today. Persecution is not merely negative; God uses it to advance the gospel — the more believers are pressed, the stronger they often grow, and the more the message is suppressed, the more widely it spreads.

The Shared Life of the Early Church

Believers were united in heart and mind as though one person, spanning backgrounds usually kept rigidly apart — rich and poor, Jew and Gentile, master and slave — even members of Herod's household and ordinary fishermen becoming brothers and sisters, highly unusual for that culture. No one claimed personal ownership; resources were shared according to need. This didn't mean private property vanished entirely — giving was voluntary and heartfelt, not coerced (later passages judge dishonest giving, confirming its voluntary nature), and proceeds from selling land mainly supported gospel ministry. This was the transitional period before the New Testament and settled doctrine existed, when believers depended heavily on the apostles' teaching; God's presence showed itself in especially visible ways, like the shaking of the meeting place. The exact model need not be copied today, but its spirit — unity, love, voluntary generosity — should be.

Applying It Today

Family is the best picture of true sharing: parents, children, and spouses share naturally. When church members truly see each other as family, not just people met on Sundays, sharing becomes natural rather than forced — through tithing, visiting those in need, and caring for the sick, rather than pressure to sell possessions.

The Resurrection as the Center of the Gospel

The apostles' central, boldest testimony was the resurrection of Jesus Christ — over 500 witnessed his ascension, a powerful historical testimony that remains the center of evangelism today. Paul writes that if Christ has not been raised, faith is futile and believers are to be pitied above all people; this life is only temporary preparation, and eternal life is the true life — the proportion between the two like the earth to the whole universe. Evangelism should not center on promises of a better marriage, business success, or an easier life; even when people first come to church because of hardship, the resurrection must remain the central message proclaimed.
