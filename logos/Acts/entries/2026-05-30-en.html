Acts 4:32-5:11 — Barnabas's Generosity & Ananias and Sapphira's Deception

You have not lied just to human beings but to God. Acts 5:4

This session contrasted Barnabas's genuine generosity with the fatal deception of Ananias and Sapphira — their sin was not withholding money, but lying to the Holy Spirit — and drew out principles for how a church should help its members today.

Early Church Community Life

Acts 4:32-35 describes a transformed community sharing possessions and meeting each other's needs — many Jerusalem Christians were living as refugees after Pentecost, creating a real, temporary need for communal support. Barnabas is introduced here, known as "son of encouragement," who sold all he had and gave the proceeds to the apostles.

Principles for Church Assistance Today

Paul's guidelines in 1 Timothy 5 still apply: able-bodied believers are expected to work diligently rather than rely on church support; family responsibility comes first, before church assistance; help involves more than money — building real spiritual fellowship matters just as much; for ongoing support, a church should consider a person's lifestyle, reputation, and willingness to receive spiritual guidance; and church assistance should target basic needs, not luxury.

The Sin of Ananias and Sapphira

The couple sold property but secretly kept back part of the proceeds while claiming to give the full amount — motivated by jealousy of the honor Barnabas received, wanting the same praise without the same sacrifice. Peter's confrontation makes the real issue clear: the property was theirs before and after the sale — giving only part would have been entirely acceptable. The actual sin was lying to the Holy Spirit, not to people — deceiving God through hypocrisy. Both died immediately after Peter exposed the deception.

Why Immediate Judgment?

The early church was in a foundational period, with no complete Scripture yet to guide believers on right and wrong; the Spirit worked powerfully and visibly through signs, wonders, and severe discipline. This immediate judgment demonstrated God's holiness and the seriousness of sin to a still-forming church. The result was great fear seizing the whole church — not terror, but a reverent recognition that faith requires genuine sincerity.

The First Use of "Church"

Acts 5:11 marks the first appearance of the word "church" (Greek ekklesia) in Acts — meaning a group of people called out by God for a common purpose, not a physical building.

Did Ananias and Sapphira Lose Their Salvation?

The group discussed this open question, drawing on similar cases (such as the man struck down for touching the Ark of the Covenant). Physical death as judgment doesn't necessarily mean loss of salvation — no one can judge another's eternal destiny, since God alone knows the heart. This passage emphasizes the severity of sin and God's holiness, not a verdict on eternity; Christians can face serious discipline or testing (as Job did) without losing their salvation.
