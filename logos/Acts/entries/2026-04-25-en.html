Acts 4:1-14 — Before the Sanhedrin: Jesus the Cornerstone

Salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given to mankind by which we must be saved. Acts 4:12

After healing a lame man at the temple, Peter and John were arrested and brought before the Sanhedrin the next day. Filled with the Spirit, Peter boldly declared that the officials had crucified Jesus, but God raised him from the dead — quoting the Old Testament to proclaim Jesus as "the cornerstone" and the only way of salvation. The miracle was undeniable, and the officials were astonished at the courage and wisdom of these "unschooled, ordinary men."

The Beginning of Persecution

Following on from chapter 3 — Peter and John healing a man lame from birth for over 40 years, and about 5,000 believing after Peter's sermon — the priests, the temple guard captain, and the Sadducees arrived, disturbed that Peter was preaching the resurrection. The Sadducees did not believe in resurrection, angels, or the spiritual — only the present world — and it was they, not the Pharisees, who held temple authority. Because it was evening, Peter and John were held overnight.

Before the Sanhedrin

The rulers, elders, scribes, and the high priests Annas and Caiaphas gathered — the very same people who had sentenced Jesus to death only months before, now attempting to intimidate his disciples. Their question: "By what power or what name did you do this?" — name representing authority. Peter, immediately filled again with the Spirit (a continuous filling after Pentecost, not a one-time event), turned the question back on them: are we on trial for a good deed done to a disabled man? Then he declared plainly: it was by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom they crucified but God raised from the dead.

Jesus as the Cornerstone

Peter quotes Psalm 118: "the stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone." A cornerstone determines the stability and direction of an entire structure — get the foundation wrong and the whole building is at risk. Jesus is the only foundation; we build our lives on him and his character. And just as a building has only one cornerstone, there is only one Jesus, one way of salvation — being raised in a Christian family, attending church, or even ordained ministry cannot substitute for actually believing in him.

The Officials' Dilemma

The officials saw Peter and John as unschooled, ordinary men — fishermen from the lower class — yet were astonished by their courage, wisdom, and grasp of Scripture, recognizing them as men who had been with Jesus. The miracle itself was undeniable: the healed man stood before them praising God, in plain view of everyone. Yet they were unwilling to accept what it pointed to — caught in an awkward, unresolved dilemma.

Transformation Through the Spirit

Two months earlier, this same Peter had denied the Lord three times and fled. Now, filled with the Spirit, he faces officials with power over his life without any fear — the Spirit giving him courage, wisdom, and power, making him a new creation. Miracles themselves cannot change a heart; only the truth of God's Word can. The officials saw the same miracle and still did not believe — just as Jesus' own miracles did not stop the leaders of his day from crucifying him. At Pentecost, 3,000 believed; here the number grew to 5,000, because of Peter's message, not the miracle alone.

Persecution and Growth

Persecution does not stop the kingdom's expansion — it tends to accelerate it. Underground churches grew explosively during the Cultural Revolution; what actually hinders growth is materialism and complacency, visible today in the decline of many Western churches.

The Spirit, Old Testament and New

The Spirit has worked together with the Father and Son since creation. In the Old Testament, the Spirit moved specific people for specific purposes and periods — David, Elijah, the prophets — inspiring David's psalms and the prophets' words. After Pentecost, the Spirit dwells in the heart of every believer, not just select individuals; indwelling (at salvation) and being filled (empowerment) are distinct experiences, and Pentecost itself was not a one-time event — it has recurred throughout church history, as in later Great Awakenings. God's plan unfolds progressively, like light widening from a narrow opening: Jesus, while on earth, was limited to one place; after his ascension, the Spirit descended into every believer's heart, enabling them to do even greater things — Father planning, Son accomplishing, Spirit revealing, the Trinity working as one throughout.

  • Practice: seek to be filled with the Spirit through a holy life and seeking God's will — not by chasing external signs like miracles or tongues, but by staying grounded in Scripture, testing any dream or vision against it.
  • Prayer focus: for believers suffering persecution in prison, trusting that the church will still grow.
