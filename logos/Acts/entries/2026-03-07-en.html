Jewish Festivals, Pentecost & the Gift of Tongues

And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit gave them utterance. Acts 2:4

This session traced how the spring festivals among the seven Jewish feasts foreshadowed Christ's redemptive work, then moved into Pentecost as a turning point and Paul's teaching on tongues.

Jewish Festivals as Types

Passover prefigured Jesus as the Passover Lamb; Unleavened Bread prefigured his sinless life and burial; Firstfruits prefigured his resurrection; and Pentecost prefigured the descent of the Holy Spirit and the start of the church age. Jesus fulfilled these Old Testament types, marking the shift from the Mosaic Law era to the era of the Spirit — the old festivals no longer needing to be observed.

Pentecost as a Turning Point

Luke wrote Acts with a physician's rigor, meticulously recording how Jesus fulfilled Old Testament prophecy. The disciples had already seen many miracles under Jesus, who had explained the Spirit's role to them. Pentecost changed how the Spirit worked: previously temporary and task-specific, from Pentecost on the Spirit's presence became permanent — dwelling in believers in a far more manifest way, seen clearly in the 120 disciples.

Two Kinds of Tongues

A key distinction was drawn between private tongues — prayer to God, personal devotion, possibly an angelic language, for praise and worship — and public tongues — evangelism to unbelievers, understandable local languages used for witness and mission. The tongues spoken at Pentecost were of the second kind: real, understandable local languages, not private prayer tongues. Observers from around the world were amazed to hear the disciples praising God and telling of Christ in their own native languages.

Paul's Teaching on Tongues

Drawing on 1 Corinthians 14: tongues belong in private prayer; in public or church settings, unintelligible tongues should be avoided so as not to confuse unbelievers. Tongues are a gift of the Spirit, not something achieved by personal effort, and — being a sovereign gift from God — cannot be used to judge a Christian's spiritual maturity.

Church Revival Movements

The early church needed special gifts of the Spirit to witness for God through hard times. Several global revival movements since the 20th century were mentioned, with more detailed historical cases promised for a future session — every believer should desire to experience revival and pursue a holier life.

Peter's Transformation

Peter's own life illustrates the Spirit's power: from an uneducated fisherman to a powerful preacher, able to quote Scripture and grasp Old Testament prophecy deeply — a transformation entirely the Spirit's work, not personal effort, showing what believers can experience when they surrender to God.
