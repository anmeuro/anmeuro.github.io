Acts 12:20-25 — Herod's Death and the Word That Keeps Spreading

But the word of God continued to spread and flourish. Acts 12:24

The group completed Acts chapter 12, finishing with Herod Agrippa's prideful death and the bridge verse that turns the whole book toward Paul's missionary journeys. The session closed with a book recommendation — a modern account that reads like a continuation of Acts — and prayer requests for the persecuted church.

Herod's Pride and Death

Acts 12:20-24 describes Herod's confrontation with the people of Tyre and Sidon. These coastal peoples depended on Herod's territory for their food supply, so despite the hostility they sought peace with him. During his public speech they flattered him, declaring, "This is the voice of a god, not of a man" — and Herod accepted the worship without deflecting any glory to God. An angel of the Lord struck him down immediately; he was eaten by worms and died. This account is corroborated by the Jewish historian Josephus, making it a historically documented event — cited in the study as evidence of the Bible's reliability. Verse 24 then states the deliberate contrast: "But the word of God continued to spread and flourish." Persecutors fall; the gospel advances.

God's Judgment — Now and in Eternity

The discussion acknowledged that not all wicked people face immediate earthly judgment. Herod's case was an exceptional, public demonstration of divine sovereignty to validate the early church. Some evildoers appear to prosper in this life — even the Psalms wrestle openly with this tension. The Bible's answer is that this life is temporary; final judgment comes in eternity, at the Great White Throne. God's sovereignty operates across both timeframes — some judgment is immediate, some is deferred, but none is escaped.

The Bridge Verse: Acts 12:25

The final verse serves as a structural hinge between the two major halves of Acts. Barnabas and Saul had traveled to Jerusalem to deliver financial support to the church there during famine and persecution; having completed that mission, they returned — bringing John Mark with them. From here the narrative shifts: Acts 1-12 is centered on Peter, while Acts 13 onward is centered on Paul.

Book Recommendation: The Heavenly Man

The session introduced The Heavenly Man, the true story of a Chinese house church evangelist who was imprisoned repeatedly for his faith over many years. In one miraculous escape, prison guards were temporarily blinded and he walked out of the prison — physically impossible under normal circumstances. He later left China and has since traveled the world preaching the gospel. The book became a global bestseller, translated into many languages with well over a million copies sold, and is often described by readers as a "modern Acts" — directly paralleling the themes of this study. Some have questioned the author's account, while churches around the world have broadly received him; the teacher has known the author personally for over twenty years and vouches for him as a humble, genuine man of God whose testimony is trustworthy.

  • Prayer request: Pastor Wang Yi of Chengdu's Early Rain Covenant Church, now about eight years into a nine-year prison sentence, is reportedly suffering from late-stage liver cancer without adequate medical care. The group prays for his healing, proper treatment, and early release.
  • Monthly fasting prayer: The 9th of every month (China time) is set aside as a fasting prayer day for persecuted churches and pastors in China — members are encouraged to fast one or two meals, or a full day.
