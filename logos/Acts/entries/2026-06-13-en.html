Acts 5:12-32 — Signs and Wonders, and Obeying God Rather Than People

We must obey God rather than human beings! Acts 5:29

The apostles perform numerous signs and wonders in Jerusalem, the church grows rapidly despite Sadducean opposition, and after a miraculous jailbreak by an angel, they keep preaching in the temple, declaring their allegiance to God over human authority.

Prayer Answered Through Signs and Wonders

These miracles fulfilled the apostles' own earlier prayer request — to perform signs and wonders in Jesus' name — after their first imprisonment. People brought the sick into the streets hoping even Peter's shadow might heal them, and all who came from surrounding towns were healed.

Faith, Not Magic

The shadow, garment, or handkerchief carries no power of its own — this parallels the woman who touched Jesus' garment and was healed by faith, and Paul's handkerchief later used for healing. Jesus consistently said, "your faith has healed you" — the object is irrelevant; what matters is faith directed at Jesus. This corrects any notion of Christian healing as magic or superstition — the focus stays on knowing God and trusting his power.

Growth and Purification

After God's judgment on Ananias and Sapphira, great fear came on the church — deterring casual followers and miracle-seekers, while genuine believers kept being added in growing numbers. The fear distinguished superficial interest from sincere faith.

The Source of Apostolic Boldness

The apostles were eyewitnesses who had personally seen Jesus' ministry, death, and resurrection, and had themselves experienced the Spirit's transforming power. Peter, once an uneducated fisherman, shows this transformation completely.

Two Kinds of Fear

The religious leaders feared losing power, influence, and position — a fear that only drove further opposition. The apostles instead showed the fear of God, which gave them power, freedom, and peace to proclaim the gospel boldly.

The Nature of the New Life

The angel commanded the apostles to tell people "all about this new life" — forgiveness of sins, reconciliation with God, and regeneration through Christ. This transformation begins at conversion and continues through the whole of earthly life, not only after death.

An Order of Obedience

Christians submit to governing authorities and workplace leaders when their commands don't contradict God's Word — but when a human command conflicts with God's, believers must obey God rather than people.

Opposition and Divine Irony

The Sadducees, who controlled the temple and denied resurrection, angels, and spirits, grew jealous of the apostles' influence and imprisoned them — only to find the jail empty the next morning, guards still posted and doors still locked. Their concern was for their own power, not whether God was actually at work. God sending an angel to free them was itself an irony, since the Sadducees denied angels existed at all — and the empty jail echoes Jesus' own empty tomb, a sign of God's sovereign power. Like Pharaoh hardening his heart through ten plagues, these leaders refused to recognize God's work despite overwhelming evidence — unbelief here came not from a lack of evidence, but from spiritual resistance.

Peter's Bold Proclamation

Before the Sanhedrin, Peter lays out four points: the leaders were responsible for crucifying Jesus; God raised him from the dead, vindicating him as righteous; God exalted him as both King and Savior; and through Jesus, God offers repentance and forgiveness to Israel — an invitation extended even to the Sadducees themselves.

  • Modern example: a brother named Da Yu faced termination for supporting a Christian colleague's baptism testimony on LinkedIn — given one hour by HR to delete his comment or be fired. Despite his wife's pregnancy and the loss of health insurance, he chose to obey God, and his wife supported the decision.
  • Call to witness: every Christian, not only the apostles, is called to be a witness — which requires personally experiencing transformation, living the new life, and being ready to pay a real cost when opposition comes.
  • Practice: pray for God to change hearts rather than fixing on miraculous signs alone; cultivate the fear of God, not the fear of people, as the source of boldness.
