Revival Movements in Church History

And it shall come to pass in the last days, God declares, that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh. Acts 2:17, quoting Joel

A side session stepping back from verse-by-verse study to look at revival movements across church history, and how they connect to Peter's Pentecost sermon in Acts 2.

Revivals Through History

The discussion moved through the Pentecostal Revival's global influence; the 19th-century Welsh Revival, which helped inspire revivals in the United States, Korea, and China; and the 1906 Azusa Street Revival in Los Angeles — the birthplace of the modern Pentecostal movement, led by African American pastor William J. Seymour, marked by the outpouring of the Spirit, speaking in tongues, healing, and interracial worship that broke racial barriers and shaped global Pentecostalism. The group noted that God often used ordinary people — Galilean fishermen, Welsh miners, humble believers at Azusa Street — to ignite these movements, and how the American revival became the seed of a worldwide Pentecostal movement known for tongues, prophecy, and miracles.

Regional Revival Histories

In North Korea, Pyongyang once had around 2,000 churches before suppression; today an estimated 20,000–40,000 believers are thought to worship in secret. In China, the house church movement grew after foreign missionaries were expelled in the 1950s, and despite persecution during the Cultural Revolution, Christianity expanded to an estimated 100 million believers today — the group also discussed the "Back to Jerusalem" movement as part of China's own missionary vision.

Peter's Preaching and the Gift of Tongues

Peter's sermon quotes Joel's prophecy of the Spirit's outpouring (Acts 2:17–21, read aloud in the group): what the crowd witnessed was prophecy fulfilled, not drunkenness. Joel's call to repentance and his phrase "the last days" were understood as spanning the whole span from Jesus' birth to his return. In the Old Testament the Spirit came upon select individuals; in the New Testament, all believers may experience the Spirit's outpouring — Joel's prophecy has been fulfilled repeatedly through successive revivals.

End-Time Judgment

The group compared Peter's language of "the great and glorious day of the Lord" with John's detailed final judgment in Revelation, and discussed the AD 70 destruction of the temple as a partial fulfillment — with the emphasis landing on hope for everyone who calls on the name of the Lord.

Key Takeaways

  • Revival is Spirit-led renewal that transcends culture and history.
  • Azusa Street showed how the Spirit can break racial and social barriers, launching a global movement.
  • The Spirit's work unites believers across nations and generations.
  • The "last days" span the entire church age until Christ's return.
  • Believers today are called to the same faith, repentance, and readiness.
