The Kingdom of God, the Ascension & the Return of Christ

Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus... will come in the same way as you saw him go. Acts 1:11

This session traced the Kingdom of God from its Abrahamic roots to Jesus' ministry, then moved into end-times theology, the ascension, and the second coming — closing with a look at why Jesus chose ordinary Galileans as his witnesses.

The Kingdom: From Promise to Fulfillment

The disciples expected a physical, political restoration of Israel's earthly kingdom; Jesus taught a broader spiritual fulfillment transcending national boundaries, with true restoration coming at the end times, contingent on the salvation of the Gentiles. This marks the shift from Old Testament expectation to New Testament spiritual reality — God's plan encompasses all nations, not just Israel.

End Times: Vigilance and Preparation

Israel's restoration is a key end-times sign, but the exact timing remains unknown — not to Jesus in his earthly ministry, not to the apostles, not to anyone today. This mystery protects against false prophets claiming special knowledge, and calls believers to constant readiness rather than date-setting.

The Great Commission and the Spirit's Power

The Spirit's work in the disciples was two-fold: first received for personal salvation and regeneration, then given afresh for effective witness and ministry. That empowerment let them proclaim the gospel boldly despite persecution, work miracles, and cast out demons — spreading the message from Jerusalem to Judea, Samaria, and the ends of the earth. The power was never for personal gain, only for gospel proclamation, with all glory returning to God.

The Ascension: Divine Glory Revealed

The cloud at the ascension was not meteorological but represented divine glory (Shekinah), consistent with Old Testament theophanies, and 500 witnesses observed the event. Two angels in white then appeared, asking the disciples why they gazed skyward and promising Jesus would return the same way he departed — establishing the pattern for the Second Coming. Those who are raised will share in that same glory.

The Second Coming: Universal Revelation

Teaching from Revelation 1:7 emphasized that Christ's return will be universally visible — unlike Old Testament appearances limited to the chosen. For unbelievers it will be a terrifying vision of judgment and a symbol of doomsday; for believers, a day of salvation and victory, the fulfillment of hope, and reunion with the Lord they love.

Galilean Disciples: God's Chosen Vessels

The angels' address, "men of Galilee," initially carried social stigma — identifying the disciples as uneducated fishermen from a despised region. Yet Jesus deliberately chose these humble people over Jerusalem's educated elite, so that glory would return to God rather than to human wisdom or credentials. God's power does not depend on qualifications, education, or social standing; transformation comes through divine calling, not human merit.

Transformation: The Key to Divine Service

God calls people regardless of educational background or social position — the essential factor is humility and willingness to submit. Effective service follows a pattern: personal repentance, spiritual rebirth through the Spirit, a dramatic change of character, then empowerment for witness. Peter (from impulsive fisherman to bold apostle after Pentecost) and Paul (from persecutor to premier missionary after his Damascus road encounter) are the prime biblical examples — both radically transformed through an encounter with the Holy Spirit.

Israel's Restoration: Spiritual vs. Physical

An important distinction was drawn between two dimensions of restoration. Physical restoration — the return to the land, beginning in 1948 — is political and geographical, and is already partly underway. Spiritual restoration — the nation turning to God and to Jesus — is theological and salvific, still awaiting future, complete fulfillment at the end times: Israel recognizing Jesus as Messiah, the ultimate fulfillment of prophetic promise and the culmination of God's redemptive plan.
